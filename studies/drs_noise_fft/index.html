Where the DRS Noise Lives

A Fourier look at the pre-pulse samples of the test-fibre channels finds that the noise is the amplifiers', concentrated at 200–600 MHz, on top of a correctable DRS4 cell pattern — and that the constant-fraction discriminator is already at the noise limit for timing.

Setup

Raw waveforms for the 20 channels in data/channel_maps/testingfibers.json plus MCP_DS_0, 4000 events of run 1994. Each 1024-sample record is baseline-subtracted per event using the median of samples 20–380. Those same samples — 72 ns before any pulse, the pulses sitting at samples 430–470 — are the noise-only window for parts 1 and 2. Part 3 uses samples 380–990; 990 stops short of the dip in the last DRS cells.

Two things the DRS4 forces on any spectral analysis. Its cell widths vary by 10–20% around the nominal 200 ps, so an FFT on the nominal grid smears content above a few hundred MHz — fine for what follows, which lives below that. And it is a transient recorder: the noise spectrum has to come from a pulse-free window, windowed (Hann) to stop the baseline step from leaking into every bin.

1. The noise is the amplifiers'

All 19 amplified channels have σ ≈ 7.2–8.2 ADC and the same spectral shape: a broad resonance peaking near 300 MHz with a shoulder at 450 MHz, then the analogue roll-off above ~600 MHz. The two channels that differ are the unamplified MCP (σ 3.7) and Quartz_1 (σ 4.4) — flatter, with a larger share above 1 GHz, which is the DRS's own white noise. The amplifiers add roughly √(7.8² − 4.4²) ≈ 6.4 ADC of coloured noise on top of that.

An early pass reported "spectral lines at 292 and 458 MHz, 30–80× the floor". That was a metric artefact: the "floor" was the median PSD, which sits in the roll-off region, so a broad bump read as a sharp line. The band table is the honest statement.

2. A fixed pattern in cell space

Nothing is fixed in sample space — subtracting the event-mean waveform removes 1% of the variance. But the DRS4's known fixed pattern is periodic in cell, and the trigger cell (StartIndexCell, per group) moves it around in sample space every event. Realigning each event by its start cell and averaging gives a per-cell offset pattern of 2–4 ADC RMS.

This one is actionable
The pattern is residual DRS voltage-calibration error. Either redo the per-cell calibration or subtract a pattern measured from pedestal data, as done here. Combined with band-limiting the amplifier resonance, σ drops from ~7.8 to ~6.0 ADC — a 25% gain in signal-to-noise on precisely the low-amplitude channels.

3. Timing: CFD is already at the limit

For each event with an MCP pulse (1282 of 4000; MCP arrival spread 3.3 ns against the DRS trigger), the channel time is estimated two ways on the same events: the 20% leading-edge CFD used in the analysis, and a matched filter — cross-correlation with the channel's own MCP-aligned profile from drs_profiles.root, parabolic sub-sample interpolation. Both are referenced to the MCP CFD. Because a robust σ over a mixed population misleads, each amplitude bin reports the core σ (events within ±2 ns of the median) and the fraction of events in that core.

Above 400 ADC the Cherenkov fibres reach 0.18–0.29 ns and the scintillating fibres ~1.3 ns, with 88–96% of events in core; CFD and matched filter agree to within their own scatter. In the 160–400 ADC bin, 40–55% of events are already out of core, and below 160 ADC almost none are in it. That is not an estimator problem — an estimator cannot recover a time from an event that does not contain the template — it is a population question: particles that missed the fibre, or crosstalk from the 2000-ADC scintillator neighbours, are two candidates.

An artefact caught, and a guard added
The first pass claimed a 3.3 ns matched-filter resolution for GradedIndex_1 even on pure noise. Its profile has no pulse, so the "template" was noise, the correlation peaked at a constant lag, and the "resolution" was exactly the 3.3 ns MCP arrival spread. A matched filter fails silently on a bad template. The study now rejects any template whose peak is under 5σ of its own baseline — which excludes GradedIndex_0, GradedIndex_1, Quartz_1 — and drops events whose correlation peaks at a window edge.

4. Cleaning: what it buys and what it cannot

Two corrections were applied to the raw waveforms and every pulse feature recomputed on the same events (MCP present, raw amplitude above 5σ and within 4 ns of where the channel's pulses sit), the cell pattern coming from a disjoint half of the run so it cannot flatter itself. cell subtracts the per-cell offsets; cell+LP adds a zero-phase low-pass at 500 MHz aimed at the amplifier resonance.

The mean pulse per channel is built exactly as the analysis builds its _VS_ts_mcp profiles — every event, shifted by its group reference channel's 50% crossing — and the analysis's own profile from drs_profiles.root is overlaid as a check. The raw curve lands on it: for Quartz_0 the same estimator over all 23 694 events gives 14.5 ADC at bin 434, the profile's value to the decimal. (A first version of this study averaged only bright events; on dim channels those are mostly out-of-time pulses and the mean was a 40 ADC smear. Selections here are on the raw waveform, once, and reused for every treatment.)

Found on the way: the run-1994 "MCP-aligned" profiles are not MCP-aligned
variables/drs.py sets MCP_REF = "MCP_DS_0" and on the next line overwrites it with "MCP_1". Run 1994 has no MCP_1, so mcp_available is false, mcp_det is None, and _ts_mcp silently becomes _ts_ref. Every _VS_ts_mcp profile and _TS_cfd_mcp time for this run is aligned by the group reference channel only. It is good enough to make sharp profiles — the reference removes most of the trigger jitter — but it is not what the name says, and the MCP would do better (0.2 ns against ~0.8 ns).

Noise σ falls from 8.0 to 7.0 ADC with the cell correction and to 6.1 with the low-pass (medians over the amplified channels). The cell correction costs nothing. The low-pass costs 5–31% of the Cherenkov peak amplitude — those pulses have real content at the cut-off — while leaving the integral within 21% and the rise time unchanged at the 0.2 ns sample granularity. Net, the median peak-to-noise ratio goes 4 → 4 → 4.

Timing does not move. With amplitude above 5σ and within 4 ns of where the channel's pulses sit the core σ against the MCP is the same to two decimals across all three treatments; at these amplitudes the CFD is not noise-limited, and where noise would matter the events are mostly not in-time pulses (part 3).

The ringing cannot be subtracted, only filtered
If the 300 MHz ringing kept its phase across the 205 ns record, one could fit it on the pulse-free samples and remove it from under the pulse without touching the signal. It does not: extrapolating a sinusoid fitted on samples 20–380 into samples 620–990 makes the residual worse (median -13%), and the best-fit frequency scatters by 136 MHz from event to event. It is a narrow-band random process with a coherence time of a few cycles. That leaves filtering, and the filter band is the signal band — so the real fix for this noise is the amplifier, not the analysis.

Per-channel distributions of every feature under the three treatments are in results/html/Run1994/DRS/DRS_Cleaning_Channels.html, with the summary bars in DRS_Cleaning.html alongside the other DQM pages.

What to do with this

  • Correct the cell pattern in the pipeline. It is free — no signal cost — and worth ~1 ADC of σ. Measure per channel from pedestal or pre-pulse samples and subtract by cell, or redo the DRS voltage calibration.
  • Treat the 300 MHz noise as a hardware problem. It cannot be subtracted and sits on the Cherenkov signal band; a low-pass buys 15–25% in peak SNR at the cost of 7–10% of the peak, and nothing in timing. Look at the amplifier's stability and bandwidth.
  • Fix MCP_REF in variables/drs.py so tb2026 runs actually align to an MCP that exists (MCP_DS_0), then regenerate the timing pages. One line.
  • Check Quartz_1's amplifier. Its noise says it is not there.
  • Understand the out-of-time population before spending more on timing estimators: on the dim channels most bright pulses are 20–80 ns off the MCP. Split it by the scintillator channels' amplitude in the same event.
