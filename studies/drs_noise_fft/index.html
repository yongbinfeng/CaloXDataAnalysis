Where the DRS Noise Lives

A Fourier look at the pre-pulse samples of the test-fibre channels finds that the noise is the amplifiers', concentrated at 200–600 MHz, on top of a correctable DRS4 cell pattern — and that the constant-fraction discriminator is already at the noise limit for timing.

Setup

Raw waveforms for the 20 channels in data/channel_maps/testingfibers.json plus MCP_DS_1 — the MCP the analysis references its timing to, chosen per run by get_mcp_reference() — 4000 events of run 1994. Each 1024-sample record is baseline-subtracted per event using the median of samples 20–380. Those same samples — 72 ns before any pulse, the pulses sitting at samples 430–470 — are the noise-only window for parts 1 and 2. Part 3 uses samples 380–990; 990 stops short of the dip in the last DRS cells.

Two things the DRS4 forces on any spectral analysis. Its cell widths vary by 10–20% around the nominal 200 ps, so an FFT on the nominal grid smears content above a few hundred MHz — fine for what follows, which lives below that. And it is a transient recorder: the noise spectrum has to come from a pulse-free window, windowed (Hann) to stop the baseline step from leaking into every bin.

1. The noise is the amplifiers'

All 19 amplified channels have σ ≈ 7.2–8.2 ADC and the same spectral shape: a broad resonance peaking near 300 MHz with a shoulder at 450 MHz, then the analogue roll-off above ~600 MHz. The two channels that differ are the reference MCP (σ 4.6) and the entry labelled Quartz_1 (σ 4.4) — flatter, with a larger share above 1 GHz, which is the DRS's own white noise. Quartz_1 looks like an MCP because it is one: see the box below. The amplifiers add roughly √(7.8² − 4.4²) ≈ 6.4 ADC of coloured noise on top of the DRS floor.

An early pass reported "spectral lines at 292 and 458 MHz, 30–80× the floor". That was a metric artefact: the "floor" was the median PSD, which sits in the roll-off region, so a broad bump read as a sharp line. The band table is the honest statement.

2. A fixed pattern in cell space

Nothing is fixed in sample space — subtracting the event-mean waveform removes 1% of the variance. But the DRS4's known fixed pattern is periodic in cell, and the trigger cell (StartIndexCell, per group) moves it around in sample space every event. Realigning each event by its start cell and averaging gives a per-cell offset pattern of 2–4 ADC RMS.

This one is actionable
The pattern is residual DRS voltage-calibration error. Either redo the per-cell calibration or subtract a pattern measured from pedestal data, as done here. Combined with band-limiting the amplifier resonance, σ drops from ~7.8 to ~6.0 ADC — a 25% gain in signal-to-noise on precisely the low-amplitude channels.

3. Timing: CFD is already at the limit

For each event with an MCP pulse (1323 of 4000; MCP arrival spread 0.6 ns against the DRS trigger), the channel time is estimated two ways on the same events: the 20% leading-edge CFD used in the analysis, and a matched filter — cross-correlation with the channel's own MCP-aligned profile from drs_profiles.root, parabolic sub-sample interpolation. Both are referenced to the MCP CFD, and every time is first corrected by its DRS group's reference channel exactly as the analysis does. That correction is not optional: without it, channels on a different DRS board from the MCP show ~1.3 ns of extra spread that is board jitter, not detector timing. With it, the resolution is the same whichever MCP is the reference. Because a robust σ over a mixed population misleads, each amplitude bin reports the core σ (events within ±2 ns of the median) and the fraction of events in that core.

Above 400 ADC the Cherenkov fibres reach 0.17–0.29 ns and the scintillating fibres 0.40–0.49 ns, with ~90% of events in core; CFD and matched filter agree to within their own scatter. The one place the matched filter shows something is the 160–400 ADC bin of the scintillating fibres, where it puts about twice as many events in core as the CFD (36–40% against 15–19%) at similar width — it finds moderate pulses the leading-edge CFD mis-times, without improving the ones both find. In the 160–400 ADC bin, 40–55% of events are already out of core, and below 160 ADC almost none are in it. That is not an estimator problem — an estimator cannot recover a time from an event that does not contain the template — it is a population question: particles that missed the fibre, or crosstalk from the 2000-ADC scintillator neighbours, are two candidates.

An artefact caught, and a guard added
The first pass claimed a 3.3 ns matched-filter resolution for GradedIndex_1 even on pure noise. Its profile has no pulse, so the "template" was noise, the correlation peaked at a constant lag, and the "resolution" was exactly the 0.6 ns MCP arrival spread. A matched filter fails silently on a bad template. The study now rejects any template whose peak is under 5σ of its own baseline — which excludes GradedIndex_0, GradedIndex_1, Quartz400_0, Quartz400_1, Quartz400_2 — and drops events whose correlation peaks at a window edge.

4. Cleaning: what it buys and what it cannot

Two corrections were applied to the raw waveforms of the whole run and every pulse feature recomputed on the same events (the analysis's mcp_clean selection; timing and rise time additionally need a raw pulse above 5σ within 4 ns of where the channel's pulses sit). The cell pattern is derived from the first half of the run; the noise σ quoted below is from the held-out second half, and it agrees with the in-sample half to 0.03 ADC, so the split changes nothing and is kept only as the check. cell subtracts the per-cell offsets; cell+LP adds a zero-phase low-pass at 500 MHz aimed at the amplifier resonance.

The mean pulse per channel is built exactly as the analysis builds its _VS_ts_mcp profiles — every event, shifted by its group reference channel's 50% crossing and by the MCP's CFD from the same [400, 550) window with the same threshold, the float shift floored as Profile1D bins it — and the analysis's own profile from drs_profiles.root is overlaid as a check. The profile in the file was booked with --mcp-clean, and the study's raw curve, computed on those same events, reproduces it to within 0.13 ADC at every bin near the peak, on every channel — same events, same alignment, same estimator, so whatever differs between the other curves and the dashed one is the cleaning and nothing else. Getting there took two corrections to the study: a first version averaged only bright events, which on dim channels are mostly out-of-time pulses, and a second took the MCP CFD over the whole record, where events without an MCP pulse lock onto the last-cell dip and land its shape just after the peak as a spurious undershoot.

The check exposes what the profile is made of. Only 31% of events pass mcp_clean; the rest get a noise time from the CFD window and smear their fibre pulses into a plateau of a few ADC. On the mcp_clean events alone — where the three treatments are compared, so that the panel compares like with like — the same estimator gives a peak 2.1× higher — Multiclad_1 25 → 53 ADC — with the same shape, and no undershoot. The shoulder ~3 ns after the peak is present in both, so it is a feature of the pulse, not of the averaging. --mcp-clean is the analysis switch that keeps only those events.

Found and fixed on the way: the "MCP-aligned" profiles were not MCP-aligned
variables/drs.py used to set MCP_REF = "MCP_DS_0" and overwrite it on the next line with "MCP_1", an MCP that runs after 1828 do not have — so _ts_mcp silently fell back to the group reference for every tb2026 run (and, for the same reason, the Sep-2024 runs). The choice now lives in one place, channels.maps.services.get_mcp_reference(): MCP_DS_1 where the run has it, else MCP_1, else none. With real MCP alignment the profiles are sharper (Multiclad_0 FWHM 10 → 7 samples) and lower, because pulses in events without an MCP hit no longer line up — use --mcp-clean to select them.

Noise σ falls from 7.9 to 7.0 ADC with the cell correction and to 6.0 with the low-pass (medians over the amplified channels). The cell correction costs nothing. The low-pass costs 8–30% of the Cherenkov peak amplitude — those pulses have real content at the cut-off — while leaving the integral within 19% and the rise time unchanged at the 0.2 ns sample granularity. Net, the median peak-to-noise ratio goes 4 → 4 → 4.

Timing does not move. With amplitude above 5σ and within 4 ns of where the channel's pulses sit the core σ against the MCP is the same to two decimals across all three treatments; at these amplitudes the CFD is not noise-limited, and where noise would matter the events are mostly not in-time pulses (part 3).

The ringing cannot be subtracted, only filtered
If the 300 MHz ringing kept its phase across the 205 ns record, one could fit it on the pulse-free samples and remove it from under the pulse without touching the signal. It does not: extrapolating a sinusoid fitted on samples 20–380 into samples 620–990 makes the residual worse (median -14%), and the best-fit frequency scatters by 121 MHz from event to event. It is a narrow-band random process with a coherence time of a few cycles. That leaves filtering, and the filter band is the signal band — so the real fix for this noise is the amplifier, not the analysis.

Per-channel distributions of every feature under the three treatments are in results/html/Run1994/DRS/DRS_Cleaning_Channels.html, with the summary bars in DRS_Cleaning.html alongside the other DQM pages.

5. Unfolding the light: n(t) from the pulses

The measured pulse is the photon arrival profile n(t) convolved with the response h(t) of SiPM, amplifier and DRS. The Cherenkov fibres see prompt light, so their mean pulse is h(t); it was taken from them and used to unfold the others, two ways. fit: a parametric n(t) — exponential rise into an exponential decay — convolved with h(t) and fitted to the profile with χ² on the profile errors (plus a 2% shape systematic, the channel-to-channel spread of h). Nothing is divided, so noise is never amplified. NNLS: n(t) as a free histogram of arrival times constrained only to be non-negative, projected-gradient least squares stopped at χ²/ndf = 1 — non-parametric, so it can show what the exponential misses. Both work in a −6…+12 ns window around the peak, because the bright scintillating pulses droop beyond that (−13% of the peak at +40 ns) in a way the 60 ADC Cherenkov pulses do not. Three things that had to be right, or nothing fits
The response must come from the same DRS board. With h(t) from Board 2 the Board 0 scintillators sat at χ²/ndf 3 and τdecay came out 20% short; the residual inter-board jitter after the reference correction (~0.3 ns) is enough to blur a 1.4 ns edge. The pedestal before the pulse must be removed first. The profiles sit 1–5% of the peak above their far baseline in the 10 ns before the pulse; left in, it sets the onset of h(t) instead of the pulse and the fits fail at χ²/ndf ≈ 50. The window must stop before the droop. Beyond +12 ns the bright pulses are not in the regime the Cherenkov response describes. The first two were found the hard way; the script now enforces all three.

What these numbers are and are not: the shape of n(t) in relative units — absolute photon numbers would need the single-photoelectron gain per channel and a SiPM saturation correction. The Cherenkov light is taken as prompt; chromatic dispersion in a metre of fibre spreads it by a few hundred ps, so h(t) is very slightly too wide and τ correspondingly slightly under. The +3 ns shoulder in h(t) is present in every Cherenkov channel on both boards and is treated as instrumental; if it is in fact optical, the scintillator τ moves by a fraction of that.

What to do with this

  • Correct the cell pattern in the pipeline. It is free — no signal cost — and worth ~1 ADC of σ. Measure per channel from pedestal or pre-pulse samples and subtract by cell, or redo the DRS voltage calibration.
  • Treat the 300 MHz noise as a hardware problem. It cannot be subtracted and sits on the Cherenkov signal band; a low-pass buys 15–25% in peak SNR at the cost of 7–10% of the peak, and nothing in timing. Look at the amplifier's stability and bandwidth.
  • Regenerate the timing pages for the tb2026 and Sep-2024 runs now that they really align to an MCP.
  • Fix the Quartz_1 entry in testingfibers.json — it names the MCP_US_1 channel. If a fibre was meant to be there, find where it actually went.
  • Use the unfolding on the fibre types that still lack signal (GradedIndex, Quartz400) once they have it; the scintillator decay times and Sapphire's slow component are already measurable at the 2% level.
  • Understand the out-of-time population before spending more on timing estimators: on the dim channels most bright pulses are 20–80 ns off the MCP. Split it by the scintillator channels' amplitude in the same event.
